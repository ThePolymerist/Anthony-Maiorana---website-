## 1. [Europe's plastic-waste export ban starts; domestic processing economics become the next test](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-bans-plastic-waste-exports-non-oecd-countries-2026-11-20_en)

**Source:** European Commission, Directorate-General for Environment. **Published:** November 20, 2026. **Effective date:** November 21, 2026.

The EU prohibition on exporting plastic waste to non-OECD countries takes effect today. The Commission's November 20 announcement is an in-window development concerning implementation of an existing law, rather than the announcement of an entirely new policy.

The destination distinction matters. Exports to OECD countries remain possible, subject to scrutiny. This is therefore a restriction on particular export routes, rather than a universal prohibition on sending European plastic waste abroad. The Commission says about half of EU plastic-waste exports went to non-OECD destinations in 2025. [Commission announcement](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-bans-plastic-waste-exports-non-oecd-countries-2026-11-20_en).

The underlying [Waste Shipment Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/1157](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1157), supplies the legal distinction behind the dates. Article 86(3)(c) applies the relevant plastic-waste prohibition from November 21, 2026. Article 42(4) allows non-OECD countries to submit requests to receive the specified plastic waste for recycling no earlier than May 21, 2029. That is an earliest application date, not an automatic reopening of trade.

**Why it matters:** A restricted export outlet changes the alternatives available to collectors, sorters, brokers, and recyclers. My inference is that pressure will be greatest where material cannot readily meet another buyer's specification at an acceptable delivered cost. The ban alone does not establish how much additional European recycling capacity will run, whether recyclate demand will increase, or what bale prices will do.

**The Polymerist angle:** Can policy that changes where waste may go also create the economics needed to process it? An essay could trace a tonne of mixed plastic from collection through sorting, conversion, and customer qualification, identifying who pays when an export option disappears. The practical questions are usable feedstock quality, reliable offtake, operating costs, and the price of the competing virgin resin.

**Source perspective:** The Commission explains and advocates for its policy. Its announcement verifies implementation and its stated rationale; it does not independently demonstrate successful industrial outcomes.

## 2. [BASF abandons its Evonik pursuit; the strategic argument moves back to execution](https://www.basf.com/global/en/media/news-releases/2026/11/p-26-202)

**Source:** BASF. **Published and announced:** November 19, 2026. **Follow-up:** Reuters, November 20, 2026.

BASF ended its effort to acquire Evonik after two rejected non-binding cash proposals. It identified €24 per share as its offer and said the prospect of a higher valuation was not justified by its assessment of Evonik's independent prospects and risks. These are BASF's explanations for withdrawing, not a neutral appraisal of the target's value. [BASF statement](https://www.basf.com/global/en/media/news-releases/2026/11/p-26-202).

The in-window event is the withdrawal. As background, Evonik's [October 30 statement](https://www.evonik.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/10/ad-hoc-statement-board-evonik.html) said its board had declined negotiations while remaining willing to hold a confidential informational exchange. Reading both statements avoids equating unwillingness to negotiate on the proposed terms with an absence of any stated willingness to communicate.

The shareholder question also remains open. On November 20, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/rag-foundation-still-aims-cut-evonik-stake-after-basf-exit-2026-11-20/) that RAG Foundation still intended gradually to reduce its Evonik holding to 25.1%. The end of this approach does not, by itself, resolve the ownership question or demonstrate that a different transaction will follow.

**Why it matters:** An acquisition can rearrange existing businesses, distribution, and capital commitments. Whether it creates a stronger innovation institution depends on integration decisions that cannot be inferred from a proposed combination. Conversely, abandoning a deal does not prove that the capital will produce better results elsewhere. Those remain questions for subsequent investment and operating evidence.

**The Polymerist angle:** What specifically would chemical consolidation enable that the companies could not accomplish independently? Compare the proposed strategic rationale with alternative uses of capital: process improvements, customer qualification, product development, manufacturing investment, or distributions. The useful comparison would identify capabilities and measurable outcomes, rather than assume that greater scale either creates or destroys innovation.

**Source perspective:** BASF and Evonik each have an interest in defending their decisions. The Reuters follow-up adds a shareholder perspective but does not establish an independent valuation of the proposed combination.

## 3. [Braskem approaches a restructuring deadline without a reported agreement](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/brazils-braskem-investors-hold-last-minute-talks-avert-court-restructuring-2026-11-19/)

**Source:** Reuters. **Published:** November 19, 2026. **Reported deadline:** November 22, 2026. **Primary background:** Braskem's November 10 response to Brazil's securities regulator.

Reuters reported that Braskem's shareholders and creditors had not reached an agreement as of November 19, ahead of a November 22 court deadline concerning close to $11 billion of debt. The account relied on four people familiar with negotiations. It is a report of unresolved talks, not an announcement that a new court-supervised restructuring has already begun.

For background, the company's [November 10 response to the CVM](https://www.rad.cvm.gov.br/ENETCONSULTA/frmDownloadDocumento.aspx?CodigoInstituicao=1&Tela=99&descTipo=IPE&numProtocolo=1587635&numSequencia=1112341&numVersao=1) describes the existing extrajudicial process and distinguishes negotiating milestones from the updated plan. It says indicative proposals remained non-binding. The primary statement is older than this reporting window; it supports context, not the later status of negotiations.

This distinction is consequential for interpreting the news. Financing negotiations, an existing extrajudicial process, and a possible subsequent court-supervised outcome are different states. The latest report does not establish a finalized deal or which plants, products, or obligations might ultimately change. No later primary resolution was verified for this briefing.

**Why it matters:** For a chemical producer, financing arrangements influence the room available for maintenance, working capital, customer commitments, and investment. Those are potential operating consequences to investigate; the debt headline alone does not establish a supply interruption, a closure, or failure to pay an individual supplier.

**The Polymerist angle:** How does a financial restructuring preserve a functioning chemical manufacturing system? A useful follow-up would distinguish changing ownership of claims from funding the assets and operating decisions that keep products available. The test is what the agreed plan actually provides, rather than whether the announcement sounds like a balance-sheet reset.

**Source perspective:** Current negotiating status comes from Reuters' confidential sources. The primary document is a company response to its regulator. The final terms and court outcome remain unverified here.
